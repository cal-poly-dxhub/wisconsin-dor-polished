# Query #13: What information is used to determine my assessment?

## Response

## Information Used to Determine Your Assessment

According to the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual (WPAM) 2021](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2021-caad7b0), assessors are required to develop assessments at **full market value** based on an actual view of the property or the best information available. Here are the key sources of information assessors use:

### Property Inspection
The best source of information is a direct view of the property. Assessors prioritize:
1. **An interior and exterior view** of the property (the most accurate)
2. **A view from a public area**, such as a road
3. **Information requested from the property owner**, such as construction contracts, leases, operating expenses, receipts, blueprints, or photographs
4. **Other sources**, such as sales listing information and building permits

### Sales Data
The **Real Estate Transfer Return (RETR)** is the primary source of sales data. It contains:
- Names and addresses of the buyer and seller
- Date of the transaction
- Legal description of the property
- Amount of consideration (sale price)

Assessors also collect and verify data from **real estate brokers, appraisers, lenders, newspapers, and multiple listing services**, including asking prices, listing prices, and typical market exposure times.

### Three Approaches to Value
Once data is gathered, assessors analyze it using up to three approaches:
- **Sales Comparison Approach** – Compares your property to recent sales of similar properties, with adjustments for differences in age, condition, quality, amenities, etc.
- **Cost Approach** – Based on the cost to replace or reproduce the improvements
- **Income Approach** – Based on the anticipated income the property could produce (typically used for income-producing properties)

### Assessment Date
The assessment reflects the **status of your property as of January 1** of each year, per sec. 70.10, Wis. Stats.

# Query #21: how do i appeal the classification of my land?

## Response

# Appealing Your Agricultural Land Classification

Based on the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual (WPAM) 2019](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2019-804a0ba), here is the process for appealing your land's classification:

1. **Contact your assessor** — If you disagree with the classification, your first step is to contact the assessor directly.

3. **Appeal at the Board of Review (BOR)** — If you still disagree after speaking with the assessor, you can appeal the classification at the Board of Review. The documents note that this is the opportunity to formally contest the classification.

The documents do not provide further detail on the full appeals process beyond these steps. For more complete guidance, I would recommend contacting the Wisconsin Department of Revenue or your local assessor's office directly.

# Query #22: What are the steps of the Board of Review process?

## Response

# Board of Review Process

The documents provided give some details about the Board of Review (BOR) process, but do not contain a comprehensive step-by-step outline of the full BOR hearing procedure. Here is what the documents do describe:

## Key Rules During the BOR Process

According to the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual (WPAM) 2023](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2023-71d920f):

- A property owner must **present a formal objection** to the BOR to contest their assessment
- The objector must make **full disclosure, under oath**, of all facts pertaining to value — failure to do so can result in being denied relief
- After the **first meeting of the BOR** and before its **final adjournment**, an objector may **not contact a BOR member** about the objection or provide information to a BOR member, except at a session of the BOR
- The BOR must allow **sufficient time** for a hearing to permit both the taxpayer and assessor to present their evidence

## Before the BOR

The [2026 Guide for Property Owners](doc:gov_publications-2026-property-owners-guide-22e16ab) notes that:
- The assessor is required to provide the property owner a **Notice of Changed Assessment** at least **15 days** (or **30 days** in revaluation years) before the BOR
- The assessment roll must be open for **public inspection** for a set period before the BOR

For a complete description of the BOR's members, organization, and procedures, the [2026 Guide for Property Owners](doc:gov_publications-2026-property-owners-guide-22e16ab) references **secs. 70.46 and 70.47, Wis. Stats.** as the governing statutes. You may want to consult those statutes or contact your local municipality for the full procedural details.

# Query #26: how do i become certified as an assessor?

## Response

# How to Become Certified as an Assessor in Wisconsin

Based on the [Assessor Certification in Wisconsin](doc:complex_inquiry_pages-assessor-certification-55b1d04) page and the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual (WPAM) 2022](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2022-f579408), here is what you need to know:

## Step 1: Choose Your Certification Level

There are five levels of assessor certification:
1. Assessment Technician
2. Property Appraiser
3. Assessor 1
4. Assessor 2
5. Assessor 3

Each level authorizes progressively more complex assessment work. An individual certified at a higher level may perform the duties of any lower level, **except** Assessor 3, which is limited to duties specific to that level.

## Step 2: Register for and Pass the Certification Exam

Certification is achieved by **passing a written exam**. According to the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual 2011](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-vol-1-2011-1182817):

- Exams are offered **quarterly**
- You must register through **Pearson VUE** (as noted on the [Assessor Certification in Wisconsin](doc:complex_inquiry_pages-assessor-certification-55b1d04) page)
- A **$20 examination fee** is required
- You must correctly answer **70% or more** of the exam questions to pass
- Simple calculators are permitted, but programmable devices are not

## Temporary Certification Option

If you need to begin working before passing the exam, you may apply for **temporary certification at the Assessment Technician level**. Key requirements include:

- You must **not have been temporarily certified previously**
- You must have a **job commitment** from an elected or appointed assessor, a contracting firm, or the Wisconsin Department of Revenue
- Temporary certification is valid for **100 days** or until the results of the next certification exam are issued, whichever comes first
- All work must be performed **under direct supervision** of the certified individual signing the assessment roll

## Additional Resources

The [Assessor Certification in Wisconsin](doc:complex_inquiry_pages-assessor-certification-55b1d04) page also references a video titled **"How to become an assessor"** (9:28 min.) available on the DOR website, which may be a helpful starting point.

# Query #32: Is Native American property taxable or exempt?

## Response

The answer depends on several factors — primarily **who owns the property**, **where it is located**, and **how it is held**. Here's a breakdown based on the [Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual (WPAM) 2024](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2024-0fe8e9e):

---

## Real Property

### Land on a Native American Reservation
Land located on a reservation and currently owned in fee simple by the tribe or a tribal member is generally **subject to state and local taxation** only in two circumstances:
- The land was previously owned in fee simple by a **non-Native American**, or
- **Congress has otherwise provided** for state and local taxation (e.g., land originally allotted under the General Allotment Act of 1887).

### Special Rule for Four 1854 Treaty Tribes
The **Bad River, Lac Courte Oreilles, Lac du Flambeau, and Red Cliff** Ojibwe bands were allotted under the 1854 Treaty. Property owned by one of these tribes or a tribal member within those reservations is **not subject to property tax** when owned in fee simple by the tribe or a tribal member — unless it was previously owned in fee simple by a non-Native American.

### Land Held in Trust by the Federal Government
All real estate **exclusively owned** by a Native American or tribe and held in trust by the Federal Government is **not taxable**, regardless of whether it is on or off the reservation.

### Non-Native American Owned Property on a Reservation
Non-Native American owned real property on a reservation is **taxable** unless an Act of Congress expressly prohibits it. The burden is on the owner to provide evidence of any federal prohibition.

---

## Personal Property

Effective with the **January 1, 2024 assessment year**, 2023 Wisconsin Act 12 created sec. 70.111(28), Wis. Stats., which **exempts personal property** (as defined in sec. 70.04) from taxation. There are limited exceptions, including property assessed as real property under sec. 70.17(3) and property subject to taxation under sec. 76.025(2).

For structures or buildings on reservation land **not held in trust**, those owned jointly by non-Native Americans and Native Americans are taxed **according to the extent of non-Native American ownership**.

---

In short, Native American property taxation is nuanced and fact-specific. The key variables are ownership, location, trust status, and treaty history.

# Query #33: what market information is applicable when determining the assessed value of large, big box, commercial property?

## Response

## Market Information for Valuing Large, Big Box Commercial Property

Based on the documents provided, here is what is relevant to determining the assessed value of large, big box commercial properties:

### Comparable Sales Selection

The [Wisconsin Supreme Court Decision: Lowe's Home Centers, LLC v. City of Delavan](doc:news_pages-assessor-news-2023-03-02-c917da3) is directly on point for big box retail properties. The Supreme Court affirmed that:

- **Comparable sales should reflect properties that are occupied and operating**, with market-rate leases in place
- **Vacant or distressed ("dark") properties are not appropriate comparables** for an occupied, operating big box store
- Sales of properties that were vacant for multiple years prior to sale, in receivership, or otherwise distressed were **rejected** as comparable sales
- Acceptable comparable sales had "no duress, none were bank-owned or in bankruptcy, and none were exposed to the market"

The [2025 Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2025-eb21b21) reinforces this, stating that comparable sales should reflect properties **similar to the subject in age, condition, use, type of construction, location, design, physical features, and economic characteristics**.

### Property Type Context

The [2025 WPAM](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2025-eb21b21) identifies **power centers** as the relevant commercial category for big box retailers, describing them as:

> "Generally comprised of several large 'big box' retailers ranging in size from 25,000 to 100,000 square feet each (e.g. Home Depot, Best Buy). They often share one centrally located parking area."

It also notes that big box retailers may be **stand-alone facilities** with their own parking lots, rather than part of a power center.

### Valuation Approaches

Per the [2018 WPAM](doc:wpam-wisconsin-property-assessment-manual-2018-3f8472f), commercial property can be valued using the **cost, income, and/or sales comparison approaches**. Specifically:

- The **cost approach** is frequently used for new construction or special purpose properties, or when sales and income/expense data are not readily available
- The **income approach** is important because buyers and sellers of commercial properties typically base transaction decisions on **net operating income**
- The assessor must be familiar with the **physical and economic characteristics** of commercial property sales

### Statutory Requirement

Under **sec. 70.32(1), Wis. Stats.**, the assessor must consider:
- Recent arm's-length sales of the property, if they conform to recent arm's-length sales of reasonably comparable property
- All factors that, according to professionally acceptable appraisal practices, affect the value of the property

---

In summary, for big box commercial properties, the key market information includes **sales of similarly occupied, operating properties with market-rate leases**, economic data supporting the **income approach**, and cost data where applicable — while specifically **avoiding** the use of dark store or distressed property sales as comparables.
